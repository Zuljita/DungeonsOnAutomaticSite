Drop the bestiary into your world.

The whole monster library as a Foundry compendium: three hundred–odd Actors for the GURPS Game Aid, each with its portrait, a hex token cut to the creature's real footprint, its attacks, and its armour on the hit locations its own anatomy calls for. One manifest URL, no account, nothing to buy.

How to install Browse the bestiary first

Install it

Foundry installs modules from a manifest URL. Copy this one:

https://assets.dungeonsonautomatic.com/monsters/enraged-eggplant/foundry/module.json
  1. Open Foundry and go to the Add-on Modules tab on the setup screen.
  2. Click Install Module.
  3. Paste the URL into the Manifest URL field at the bottom of the dialog — not the search box.
  4. Click Install.
  5. Launch your world, then enable Dungeons on Automatic Monster Library (GURPS) under Game Settings → Manage Modules.

The monsters appear under Compendium Packs as DOA Monsters (GURPS). Drag one onto the canvas and it arrives with its token already sized to its footprint.

You need two things first. Foundry VTT version 13 or later, and the GURPS Game Aid system installed in the world you want to use it in. The Game Aid is a free, unofficial community system; this module holds monster data for it and does not work without it.

What lands in your world

Every Actor carries the same set, generated from the same reviewed library the bestiary and the dungeon generator use.

Art on the sheet and the map

The portrait sits on the Actor. The token is the flat-top hex cut, sized from the creature's stated footprint — a fourteen-hex aboleth occupies fourteen hexes rather than a square you eyeball.

Stats that resolve

Attributes, HP and FP, Basic Speed and Move, every attack with its skill, damage and reach, and the full trait and skill lists. Attacks that allow no attack roll stay as notes rather than pretending to be weapons.

Armour where it belongs

Each creature's DR sits on the hit locations its own anatomy calls for. A naga gets the six-location vermiform table, a dragon gets wings and hind legs, an octopus gets numbered arms.

Credit that travels

Every Actor carries a note naming its content licence and the originator credit, so the attribution stays attached to the monster rather than living only in a readme.

Updating. Actor IDs are derived from the monster, not from the build, so reinstalling a newer version updates the monsters already in your world instead of duplicating them.

Why it isn't in Foundry's module browser

Because the artwork is AI-generated, and Foundry's package listing does not accept that.

Foundry's AI Content Policy requires prepared images and text in listed packages to originate from human creative work. The portraits and tokens here were generated, and every prompt behind all nine hundred–odd images is recorded in the library's public art manifest. Rather than misrepresent that, the module is distributed directly — which the same policy explicitly preserves the right to do.

The practical difference is one step: you paste a URL instead of searching a list. Everything after that is identical, updates included.
