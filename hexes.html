Hexes on Automatic · the overworld sibling

The region around the dungeon.

Point it at a seed and get a GM-usable hex region: terrain, rivers, settlements, dungeon sites, landmarks, roads with travel times, and the first sketch of the regional powers. Optional local AI names and describes it. It writes an open Campaign Vault that Dungeons on Automatic plugs into.

What it makes

Everything below is deterministic: same seed, same region. The model steps are optional and always have a named fallback.

Terrain and water

Elevation, moisture and temperature fields become a closed vocabulary of terrain per hex — climate band × terrain → biome — with coasts and downhill-traced rivers.

Settlements and sites

Scored settlement placement (river, coast, terrain, spacing), dungeon sites kept away from towns, landmark hexes, and terrain-derived faction stubs with a relationship sketch.

Roads and travel

Settlements joined along a spanning trunk network routed over terrain cost, fords and bridges where roads meet rivers, and a travel table in miles and walking days.

Obstacles and residents

Passes, cliffs, bogs, scree, tolls and dry stretches seeded from the geometry; encounter and lair candidates drawn from the public monster library by climate and terrain.

Two directors, if you want them

Aldrith Vane, the cartographer, names the region and its places and writes the gazetteer notes from the drafting table. Bram Half-Ear, the caravan master, names the roads, hazards and crossings and stages the encounters from the ground. Each is a local model choosing from closed lists the deterministic layer prepared — every pick is validated, every reject echoes back, and with no model configured you get the deterministic names instead. Uses the same built-in local AI setup as Dungeons on Automatic: one click downloads a small model onto the app's own llama.cpp runtime, or connect your Ollama, LM Studio or llama.cpp server.

Make your first region

  1. Install and launch. Pick your platform above and follow the first-run steps for unsigned builds.
  2. Generate. Enter a seed (or keep the one offered) and set the size, water and ruggedness knobs. The map, the hex inspector and the road/travel sidebar fill in immediately.
  3. Optionally, set up Local AI from the sidebar and click Polish Region to let Aldrith and Bram name and describe what the generator laid out.
  4. Export to Vault. Choose a folder (new or an existing Campaign Vault). Hexes writes regions/<region>/region.md, one note per content hex, settlement/site/faction stubs, its own state under state/, and a commissions/<site>.request.json work order for every dungeon site — the envelope Dungeons on Automatic can fill in. Open the folder in Obsidian; your own edits outside the generated fences survive re-export.
  5. Come back to it with Open from Vault, which lists the regions saved in a vault and reopens one.

Without the desktop shell (browser mode) you can still generate, polish, and Save JSON / Open JSON; Export to Vault and Open from Vault need the desktop app, because only it writes to your folders.
