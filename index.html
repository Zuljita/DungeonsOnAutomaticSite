How it works

A generator that remembers you have to run this thing.

DOA handles the spatial work, content passes, and export plumbing. The judgment calls stay yours, because they only make sense with your players in the room.

01 / BRIEF

Set the intent

Choose the scale, danger, source books, tone, and any set pieces the evening requires. Party CER keeps encounters sized to your delvers.

02 / BUILD

Generate the place

Biomes, organic zones, and room geometry become a connected dungeon from one deterministic seed. Same seed and settings, same dungeon.

03 / REVIEW

Inspect the trouble

Read the adventure frame and room key. Check exits, CER, hazards, treasure, and hidden routes. Reroll the parts that miss.

04 / EXPORT

Take it to the table

Send players the clean map. Keep the secrets, procedures, encounter notes, and treasure to yourself.

Local AI, fenced in

AI imagination, made to follow the rules.

DOA’s directors run on a local model — the app can download one and run it on its bundled llama.cpp runtime, or connect to the Ollama, LM Studio, or llama.cpp server you already have. They interpret your brief and improve the narrative, but every action travels through a typed tool the generator understands. No model, no problem: the deterministic generator runs fine without one.

Director request pipelineGuardrails active
01 / Ground

Your party and library set the facts: CER, owned source books, DFRPG data, tone, tags, and requested set pieces.

02 / Propose

Local AI turns the brief into intent: loose notes become structured suggestions and director actions.

03 / Build

Deterministic systems do the physical work: partitions, zones, rooms, connections, encounters, treasure, and player-safe visibility.

04 / Verify

Validators get the last word: they reject impossible geometry, broken references, and disallowed mutations before the result reaches the dungeon.
